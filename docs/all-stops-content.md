# Rails & Roots: complete stop content pack

**Route:** Pretoria → Johannesburg Park → Cape Town  
**Content status:** Narrative content frozen for the hackathon build  
**Last research pass:** 24 September 2026  
**Traveller used in the app:** Lerato  

## 1. How to use this pack

This is the final content specification for every point shown on the Rails & Roots route. It closes the open-ended stop-research phase. Missing opening hours, prices, access arrangements and business confirmations are listed at the end as operational checks; they are not reasons to keep rewriting the route.

The product follows one continuous traveller story. Lerato begins in Pretoria, uses Gautrain to reach Park Station, changes to the simulated historic/proposed Johannesburg–Cape Town corridor and moves through the country's mining, river, farming, railway, Karoo and coastal stories.

The long-distance leg is not presented as a currently bookable service. Until PRASA publishes a current timetable and booking channel, the app must use one consistent label:

> **Historic corridor journey simulation — live passenger service not currently confirmed.**

## 3. Complete journey content

## Stop 1 — Pretoria

**Tier:** Featured  
**Story title:** *Where the country starts explaining itself*  
**Core question:** What does a capital city choose to remember?

### Lerato's arrival story

Lerato begins at Pretoria Station. It is her first time attempting the full Rails & Roots journey, so the app does not assume she understands the interchange. It confirms her selected route, explains that the first leg uses Gautrain and shows Park Station as the place where the long-distance story begins.

Pretoria is introduced through power, memory and movement. Government buildings may dominate the city's national image, but the chapter must show that a capital is also shaped by workers, commuters, students, neighbourhoods and contested histories. Pretoria Main Station, Freedom Park on Salvokop and the city's museums place different versions of South Africa within the same urban landscape.

### Content and experiences

- **Freedom Park:** national memory and panoramic context. A local transfer is required.
- **DITSONG National Museum of Natural History:** biodiversity, archaeology and human-origins collections. Confirm hours before publication.
- **Melrose House Museum:** nineteenth-century urban and conflict history. Confirm current public access.
- **Station precinct:** use for orientation and transport history, not unsupported claims about safe walking routes.

### App treatment

Lerato receives a short orientation: **Gautrain Pretoria → Centurion → Midrand → Sandton → Rosebank → Park**. She taps three themes—*power*, *memory* and *movement*—to form the opening question she will revisit in Cape Town.

**Narration line:**

> “Pretoria is not just where the train starts. It is where the country starts explaining itself.”

**Mobility and publication rule:** Display only operator-confirmed Gautrain information. Attractions are local-transfer experiences unless a safe route has been checked.

## Stop 2 — Centurion

**Tier:** Transit moment  
**Story title:** *The city lifts onto the viaduct*

Centurion is not treated as a rushed tourist excursion. Its purpose is to make the first movement of the journey visible. Gautrain's station sits on an elevated section of the system, giving the app an opportunity to explain how transport infrastructure crosses roads, businesses, housing and older settlement patterns.

**Lerato-facing line:**

> “The capital is behind you, but the city has not ended. Gauteng stretches through connected centres rather than one neat skyline.”

**App action:** A ten-second window card shows the train moving from ground-level Pretoria into an elevated urban corridor. The user may save Centurion for a future local trip, but no attraction detour is pushed during this journey.

**Status:** Verified as a Gautrain route point. No stop-specific tourism recommendation is required for the MVP.

---

## Stop 3 — Midrand

**Tier:** Transit moment  
**Story title:** *A city growing between cities*

Midrand tells the story of development between Pretoria and Johannesburg. Gautrain places the station on the Old Pretoria–Johannesburg Road beside Grand Central Airport. The app should use observable urban change—offices, roads, logistics, housing and open land—to ask how fast-growing areas acquire identity.

**Lerato-facing line:**

> “Midrand was once described by what it stood between. Growth has turned the space between two cities into a centre of its own.”

**App action:** A then-and-now urban-growth slider. It is an explanatory illustration, not a claim that every building shown can be seen from the train.

**Status:** Station location verified. Keep it as a route-story moment rather than inventing a station-adjacent attraction.

---

## Stop 4 — Sandton

**Tier:** Transit moment  
**Story title:** *Money above, movement below*

Sandton Station is deep beneath the commercial centre. That contrast gives the chapter its purpose: the user passes underneath one of the country's most visible business districts while travelling on public transport. The story can discuss economic concentration without reducing Johannesburg to wealth or presenting inequality as sightseeing.

**Lerato-facing line:**

> “Above the platform is a skyline built to display success. Below it, thousands of ordinary journeys keep that skyline moving.”

**App action:** A vertical animation moves from the underground platform to the skyline and back to commuters. Any nearby retail or public-space recommendation requires a fresh walking and opening-hours check.

**Status:** Verified Gautrain station; no unsourced business partnership or attraction card.

---

## Stop 5 — Rosebank

**Tier:** Transit moment  
**Story title:** *Neighbourhoods have their own rhythm*

Rosebank Station is beneath Oxford Road and opposite The Zone. It provides a short transition from the scale of Sandton to a denser neighbourhood associated with art, retail, food and everyday movement. Rails & Roots should avoid turning a shopping centre into the whole identity of Rosebank.

**Lerato-facing line:**

> “A route is not only the distance between cities. It is the rhythm of the neighbourhoods underneath your feet.”

**App action:** An audio transition blends station sounds with a short neighbourhood pulse. The stop remains optional content so Lerato is not overwhelmed before the Park Station interchange.

**Status:** Station position verified; destination listings need live checks.

---

## Stop 6 — Johannesburg Park

**Tier:** Featured  
**Story title:** *The hinge of the journey*  
**Core question:** How has movement built Johannesburg?

### Lerato's interchange story

Park Station is the first major action in the user journey. Lerato leaves one rail system and must locate another. The app clearly distinguishes **Gautrain Park Station** from **Johannesburg Park Station**, explains that they are adjacent rather than the same platform and never promises a timed connection that has not been verified.

The city chapter links physical interchange to Johannesburg's wider story of migration, mining, labour and reinvention. People came to the city for gold, work, trade, education and refuge; apartheid attempted to control where those people could live and move. Constitution Hill gives the app a responsibly interpreted route from incarceration to constitutional democracy.

### Content and experiences

- **Constitution Hill:** rights, incarceration and democracy; local transfer required.
- **Museum Africa and Newtown:** city and social history, subject to current opening confirmation.
- **Braamfontein and Newtown:** contemporary urban culture through a curated daytime list, not a vague instruction to wander.
- **The interchange itself:** step-by-step orientation, assistance points and a generous connection buffer once real services exist.

### App treatment

The map pauses. Lerato completes an interchange checklist: *exit Gautrain*, *follow verified directions*, *confirm the correct departure information* and *board only when live operator data confirms the service*. The city story unlocks while she transfers.

**Narration line:**

> “Every big journey has a hinge. Here, the hinge is Johannesburg—a city built by people arriving, leaving and beginning again.”

**Mobility and publication rule:** Never describe the transfer as cross-platform. Do not provide platform numbers, walking times or long-distance departure details without current operator confirmation.

## Stop 7 — Krugersdorp

**Tier:** Secondary  
**Story title:** *Where the metropolis meets older landscapes*

Krugersdorp introduces the West Rand: a place shaped by mining and city expansion but also connected to indigenous biodiversity and the wider Cradle region. This stop should not retell Johannesburg's gold story. Its purpose is to show how extraction, suburban growth and protected landscapes exist beside one another.

**Main experience:** Walter Sisulu National Botanical Garden, known for its waterfall, indigenous planting and Verreaux's eagle habitat. It requires a local transfer and current visitor information.

**App action:** The user changes the soundscape from mine machinery and traffic to water and bird calls, then answers: *What survives when a city expands?*

**Status:** The botanical garden is the strongest publishable anchor. Game-reserve claims remain excluded until current access is confirmed.

---

## Stop 8 — Potchefstroom

**Tier:** Secondary  
**Story title:** *A town where learning changes the landscape*

Potchefstroom brings together the Mooi River, university life, historic buildings, sport and green space. The chapter should recognise that a university town is shaped both by long-established residents and by people who arrive temporarily to study, work and compete.

**Main experiences:** North-West University Botanical Garden; Potchefstroom Museum and historic house museums, subject to operating checks.

**App action:** Lerato chooses one of three voices—*student*, *long-time resident* or *river*—and sees how each experiences the same town differently. These are editorial perspectives, not fabricated quotations from real people.

**Narration line:**

> “Some towns grow around mines or junctions. Potchefstroom also grows around people who arrive to learn—and take part of the town with them when they leave.”

**Status:** Complete story; confirm museum hours, mobility and a current locally owned food listing before public release.

---

## Stop 9 — Klerksdorp

**Tier:** Secondary  
**Story title:** *More than a gold town*

Klerksdorp can easily become another mining paragraph, so its chapter deliberately widens the frame. The Klerksdorp Museum can connect mining and town development with Khoisan rock-art and Korana engraving displays. This lets Rails & Roots show that the region's human history did not begin when minerals became commercially valuable.

**Main experiences:** Klerksdorp Museum; Faan Meintjies Nature Reserve as a transfer-based excursion.

**App action:** A layered timeline begins before mining, then adds mineral extraction, railway movement and the modern city. The oldest layer never disappears beneath the newest one.

**Narration line:**

> “Gold changed Klerksdorp, but it did not begin its story.”

**Status:** Complete story. Museum access, reserve transport and business listings require final operational checks.

---

## Stop 10 — Bloemhof

**Tier:** Secondary  
**Story title:** *When the river becomes a reservoir*

Bloemhof changes the pace of the route. The Vaal system becomes infrastructure, habitat and livelihood around Bloemhof Dam and Sandveld Nature Reserve. The stop should explain that large water projects affect people, wildlife, farming and recreation simultaneously.

**Main experience:** Bloemhof Dam and Sandveld Nature Reserve for birding, fishing, game viewing and landscape interpretation. Provincial material places the reserve outside the town, so it must be presented as an excursion.

**App action:** A water-level illustration asks the user to balance four needs: households, agriculture, ecosystems and recreation. It is educational, not a real dam-management simulator.

**Narration line:**

> “At Bloemhof, water slows down—and the journey opens into habitat, livelihood and responsibility.”

**Status:** Complete story. Verify current reserve access, conservation fees, activity rules and transfer options.

---

## Stop 11 — Christiana

**Tier:** Secondary  
**Story title:** *Diamonds carried by a river*

Christiana's diamond story differs from Kimberley's mine. Here, the Vaal River and its gravel introduce alluvial diamonds—minerals moved and sorted by water. The chapter connects discovery to settlement, land access, labour and the town's present agricultural and river identity.

**Main experiences:** Diggers' Diamond Museum, subject to direct access confirmation; Vaal River viewpoints; protected rock-engravings story only through an authorised guide or custodian.

**App action:** Lerato examines a simplified river-gravel layer to learn why heavier minerals collect in particular deposits. The app does not pretend to locate real diamonds.

**Narration line:**

> “In Christiana, the river carried more than water. What it left in the gravel changed how people valued the land.”

**Status:** Complete story. Museum hours and heritage-site access remain verification items; sensitive heritage is never gamified.

---

## Stop 12 — Warrenton

**Tier:** Secondary  
**Story title:** *Making water travel*

Warrenton changes the river story again. Instead of diamonds in gravel, the focus is organised water movement. The Vaalharts diversion weir sends water into a canal system supporting agriculture, towns and households. Fourteen Streams adds a natural and railway layer, while the Class 19D locomotive listed at the municipal grounds makes rail history visible.

**Main experiences:** Vaalharts diversion-weir interpretation; Fourteen Streams and railway history; the Class 19D locomotive, subject to current site confirmation; the broader agricultural landscape.

**App action:** The user traces one drop from river to canal, farm, market and household. The final screen asks who maintains the system and who receives access to its benefits.

**Narration line:**

> “At Warrenton, the river becomes a network—and water begins a journey of its own.”

**Status:** Complete story. Do not direct users onto railway land, farms, canals or service roads. Verify a legal viewing point and current locomotive access.

## Stop 13 — Kimberley

**Tier:** Featured  
**Story title:** *Diamonds made headlines; people made Kimberley*  
**Core question:** Who paid the human cost of mineral wealth?

### Lerato's destination story

Kimberley is the strongest interior chapter. The Big Hole and its mining museum provide an immediate visual anchor, but the story must not celebrate diamonds without context. Mining reorganised land, labour, wealth, housing and racial control. People performed dangerous work while companies and political figures accumulated power. Communities experienced dispossession and new forms of segregation that influenced urban South Africa far beyond Kimberley.

The McGregor Museum allows the chapter to move beyond the mine into archaeology, natural history, Chief Galeshewe, conflict over land and minerals, Olive Schreiner and the Siege of Kimberley. Wildebeest Kuil, if included, must be approached through its official custodians and interpreted as living Indigenous heritage—not as a decorative prelude to the diamond rush.

### Content and experiences

- **The Big Hole and Kimberley Mine Museum:** mining landscape, old-town interpretation and industrial history.
- **McGregor Museum:** regional archaeology, natural history and social history.
- **Wildebeest Kuil Rock Art Centre:** guided rock-art experience; verify current arrangements.
- **Magersfontein Battlefield:** regional excursion requiring planned transport.

### App treatment

Lerato enters a layered story rather than a treasure hunt. She chooses *land*, *labour* or *legacy*. Each path returns to the same conclusion: the value of a diamond cannot explain the full cost of obtaining it. The app may use object-based storytelling—tools, maps, contracts and photographs—but must not reward users for “collecting diamonds.”

**Narration line:**

> “The stone may be small enough to hold in one hand, but the system built around it changed an entire city.”

**Mobility and publication rule:** Use verified venue hours and fees on the demo date. Regional sites require transfers. Avoid the disputed slogan that the Big Hole is definitively the world's largest hand-dug excavation.

## Stop 14 — De Aar

**Tier:** Secondary  
**Story title:** *A town made by lines meeting*

De Aar is one of the clearest Rails & Roots stories because the railway is not merely something that passes through the town—it is central to why the town developed. Its junction role connected multiple regions, moved freight and passengers and shaped work, neighbourhoods and identity.

The story can also introduce Olive Schreiner, the author, feminist and anti-war thinker associated with the town. Tourism sources list her former house, but its present operating model must be confirmed before it is described as an open museum or restaurant. The Garden of Remembrance and regional rock engravings add conflict and much older human history, but require careful, current interpretation.

**App action:** Lerato rotates a railway-junction diagram and sees how closing or opening one line changes the towns connected to it.

**Narration line:**

> “Some towns happen beside a railway. De Aar happened because the lines met.”

**Status:** Narrative complete. Confirm the Olive Schreiner house, rock-art custodianship and safe local transport. Avoid ranking De Aar as the country's ‘second-largest’ junction unless the metric and current source are supplied.

## Stop 15 — Hutchinson

**Tier:** Secondary  
**Story title:** *The stop that points twelve kilometres away*

Hutchinson shows how rail geography can redirect the fortunes of places. Northern Cape Tourism records that the railway bypassed Victoria West by about 12 km, creating the need for a railhead that developed into Hutchinson. A branch line later linked the two.

The app must be honest: Hutchinson itself is a tiny rail settlement, while most visitor experiences are in Victoria West. That is not a weakness; it is the story. Transport infrastructure can create one settlement, bypass another and force a relationship between them.

**Main experience:** A mapped rail-to-road story connecting Hutchinson and Victoria West. Victoria West's architecture, museum and hospitality require an arranged road transfer.

**App action:** The user chooses where to place a fictional railway line and sees which settlement gains access and which must adapt.

**Narration line:**

> “The railway missed Victoria West—but the missed connection created Hutchinson.”

**Status:** Complete. Do not promise station-adjacent food, accommodation or transport; none has been reliably verified.

---

## Stop 16 — Beaufort West

**Tier:** Featured  
**Story title:** *The Karoo becomes the main character*  
**Core question:** What becomes visible when the landscape grows quiet?

### Lerato's destination story

Beaufort West gives the journey breathing room. The scale of the Great Karoo changes the traveller's sense of time and distance. Instead of filling the silence with constant facts, the app should allow a slower soundscape and a visual focus on geology, plants, animals and sky.

Karoo National Park, approximately 12 km from town, is the major natural anchor. SANParks currently lists game drives, walking, hiking, birding, mountain biking and 4×4 activities, as well as accommodation and a restaurant. It is a planned excursion rather than a station walk. In town, the Beaufort West Museum complex can connect local history to Christiaan Barnard, whose medical career became globally significant.

### Content and experiences

- **Karoo National Park:** ecology, geology, wildlife, trails and night sky; transfer and park booking required.
- **Beaufort West Museum complex:** local history and the Chris Barnard collection; confirm current hours.
- **Historic town centre:** architecture and town development through a verified guide or walking route.

### App treatment

The screen deliberately simplifies. Lerato selects a sound—wind, birds or near-silence—and looks for three landscape details before narration resumes. A night-sky mode can explain low light pollution without guaranteeing astronomical conditions.

**Narration line:**

> “The Karoo does not shout. It gives you enough silence to hear the journey.”

**Mobility and publication rule:** State the park transfer clearly. Pull entry fees, bookings, activity conditions and accommodation prices from SANParks when the user intends to travel.

## Stop 17 — Laingsburg

**Tier:** Secondary  
**Story title:** *What a town remembers after the water leaves*

Laingsburg's central story is the devastating 1981 flood, but the app must not turn loss into spectacle. The Flood Museum and walking Flood Route allow the town to speak through memory, evidence and rebuilding. The chapter connects local experience to modern questions about disaster preparedness, settlement beside rivers and changing environmental risk.

**Main experiences:** Laingsburg Flood Museum; the walking Flood Route; surrounding Karoo geology and landscape.

**App action:** The user compares an ordinary town map with the flood path and then selects what a community needs before, during and after a disaster. No points or playful badges are awarded for loss of life.

**Narration line:**

> “A flood can pass in hours. What a town remembers—and rebuilds—lasts much longer.”

**Status:** Story complete. Confirm museum hours and route conditions before travel; use survivor testimony only with consent and attribution.

---

## Stop 18 — Matjiesfontein

**Tier:** Secondary  
**Story title:** *A railway village preserved in one frame*

Matjiesfontein is one of the route's most concentrated heritage experiences. The station, Lord Milner Hotel, museums, historic streets and short red-bus tour sit within one recognisable village environment. The app should still distinguish preserved atmosphere from a complete representation of the past: Victorian design, imperial memory, railway hospitality and South African War history need context.

**Main experiences:** Red Bus Tour; Marie Rawdon Museum; Transport Museum; courthouse and jail; Lord Milner Hotel and Laird's Arms. The official village site currently lists these experiences.

**App action:** An archival overlay lets Lerato compare the station precinct across time. She selects an object from the museum collection and follows its connection to rail travel, war or hospitality.

**Narration line:**

> “Matjiesfontein looks preserved in time. The challenge is to ask whose time—and whose story—was preserved.”

**Status:** Strong, ready-made heritage product. Recheck tour schedule, museum access and whether activities are included only for overnight guests.

## Stop 19 — Worcester

**Tier:** Strong secondary  
**Story title:** *Where the landscape changes its vocabulary*

Worcester marks the transition from the long Karoo journey into a mountain-ringed agricultural valley. The Karoo Desert National Botanical Garden provides the strongest anchor because it explains how arid and semi-arid plants survive and why biodiversity does not require a green, forested landscape.

Worcester Museum and Kleinplasie can add agricultural and social history, while regional wine experiences introduce farming, labour, land and water in the Breede Valley. Wine must be presented as one part of the economy and culture, not the town's entire identity.

**App action:** The user matches plant adaptations—water storage, small leaves and dormancy—to environmental pressures. The map colours gradually change from Karoo tones to valley agriculture.

**Narration line:**

> “The mountains do not end the Karoo story; they change how water, plants and people respond to it.”

**Status:** Story complete. The botanical garden requires a local transfer. Verify museum access and chosen wine experiences; include non-alcoholic options and responsible-travel messaging.

## Stop 20 — Wellington

**Tier:** Secondary  
**Story title:** *Roots that travel*

Wellington brings together agriculture, education, wine and layered settlement history. Its nursery and farming story offers a literal version of “roots”: plant material cultivated here contributes to vineyards elsewhere. The town's heritage must include Khoe and San presence, enslaved and farm labour, mission and education histories, and later settler narratives rather than presenting a single romantic Winelands story.

**Main experiences:** Wellington Museum; official heritage trail; selected wine, food and outdoor experiences through the official destination platform.

**App action:** A vine-cutting animation follows a plant from nursery to vineyard while the story introduces the people and labour behind the landscape. A non-alcoholic content path is always available.

**Narration line:**

> “Wellington sends roots across the Winelands—but roots also mean the histories a place chooses to keep.”

**Status:** Complete. Estates and museum visits require live hours, transport and accessibility checks. Avoid claiming that a fixed percentage of South Africa's vine cuttings comes from Wellington unless a current industry source confirms it.

## Stop 21 — Huguenot (Paarl)

**Tier:** Secondary  
**Story title:** *A language made by many histories*

The first task is orientation: Huguenot is the Paarl-area passenger station in the route record; it is not the Huguenot Monument in Franschhoek. Paarl's strongest chapter is language and identity. The Afrikaans Language Museum and Monument explain a language shaped through contact among people from Africa, Europe and South-East Asia.

The app must resist a one-community ownership story. Afrikaans has diverse speakers and histories, including enslaved people, Khoe communities, Muslim scholars, colonial institutions, nationalism, apartheid and contemporary cultural life. The monument can therefore become a place for questioning how language is remembered publicly.

**Main experiences:** Afrikaans Language Museum; Afrikaans Language Monument on Paarl Mountain; Paarl Mountain landscape and selected town heritage.

**App action:** Users hear the same everyday phrase in several Afrikaans varieties and explore the language's multiple roots. Audio must come from consenting speakers and be credited.

**Narration line:**

> “A language does not grow from one mouth. It carries the journeys, power and creativity of many people.”

**Status:** Complete. Both museum and monument require verified transfers and current visitor information.

---

## Stop 22 — Bellville

**Tier:** Secondary  
**Story title:** *The everyday gateway*

Bellville is not a filler stop before Cape Town. It began as a railway-related settlement and remains a major northern-suburbs transport and commercial node. Its value in the story is connectivity: commuters, students, shoppers and residents experience metropolitan Cape Town long before the tourist postcard appears.

Tygerberg Nature Reserve provides a natural counterpoint, conserving renosterveld and offering broad metropolitan views, but it requires onward transport. Jack Muller Park and a verified Bellville heritage route can support a more local chapter.

**App action:** The user switches between three maps—rail network, residential movement and biodiversity—to see one place functioning in different ways.

**Narration line:**

> “Cape Town does not begin at Table Mountain. For thousands of people, it begins in the daily connections made through Bellville.”

**Status:** Complete. Verify reserve hours, entry conditions and transport. Do not market Tyger Valley accommodation as station-adjacent without checking the actual route.

## Stop 23 — Cape Town

**Tier:** Featured  
**Story title:** *The ocean is an arrival, not an ending*  
**Core question:** What did the railway connect—and what did it divide?

### Lerato's arrival story

Cape Town completes the mapped journey but opens the final reflection. The city chapter begins near the station with the Castle of Good Hope, Grand Parade, City Hall, District Six and the old street grid. It avoids jumping immediately to distant postcard attractions. These nearby places allow the app to discuss colonial settlement, slavery, forced removal, protest, civic life and the city's relationship with its port.

Table Mountain, Robben Island and the Cape Peninsula remain important, but they are separate excursions. Robben Island tours leave from the V&A Waterfront, depend on weather and availability and require timed booking. The app must not suggest that the island or Table Mountain is beside Cape Town Station.

### Content and experiences

- **Castle of Good Hope and Grand Parade:** colonial power, public space and changing city geography.
- **District Six Museum and area:** forced removals, memory and restitution; verify hours and booking.
- **Company's Garden and Iziko Slave Lodge precinct:** slavery, government and public memory.
- **Robben Island Museum:** separate booked excursion from the V&A Waterfront.
- **Table Mountain:** separate weather-dependent excursion with current access information.

### App treatment

Lerato's saved stories appear as a route ribbon: capital, metropolis, mining belt, rivers, irrigation, diamonds, junctions, Karoo, Winelands and port city. She revisits the opening Pretoria question and records what changed in her understanding. The completion reward is a digital route journal, not a generic confetti screen.

**Narration line:**

> “You have reached the ocean, but not the end of the story. Every arrival changes the way you understand where you began.”

**Mobility and publication rule:** Separate station-area heritage from waterfront, mountain and peninsula excursions. Show live prices, schedules and bookings only from current official providers.

## 4. Narrative flow between stops

The route must feel like one story rather than 23 unrelated cards:

1. **Pretoria to Park:** How people and power move through a modern province.
2. **West Rand and North West:** How mining, learning, rivers and agriculture shape towns.
3. **Warrenton and Kimberley:** How infrastructure and mineral wealth reorganise land and labour.
4. **De Aar to Matjiesfontein:** How railways create junctions, bypass places and carry travellers through the Karoo.
5. **Worcester to Bellville:** How the landscape narrows into mountains, farming regions and metropolitan networks.
6. **Cape Town:** How the entire inland route reaches a port city and becomes part of a larger history.

## 5. Standard app object for every stop

Every stop record should contain:

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Identity | Correct station and town name, province and route order |
| Tier | Featured, secondary or transit moment |
| Story | One title, one core idea and 60–90 seconds of narration |
| Visual anchor | A rights-cleared image of the actual place or experience |
| Action | Listen, compare, trace, reflect, save or plan |
| Mobility | Walk verified, local transfer required, excursion or access unconfirmed |
| Evidence | Source URL, access date and last operational check |
| Safety | Site-specific restriction where relevant |
| Commercial status | Discovery listing, proposed partner or confirmed partner |

## 6. Content and ethics rules from the workshop pointers

- Follow Lerato's complete journey; do not demonstrate disconnected features.
- Show real app behaviour. A button displayed in the demo must work.
- Use proper icons and a consistent interface; no emoji should appear inside the product UI.
- Never invent a partnership, fare, timetable, opening hour, route, quotation or local legend.
- Do not turn apartheid, slavery, dispossession, war, disaster or sacred heritage into a points game.
- Do not describe an attraction as walkable using straight-line distance.
- Give local people agency. Towns are living communities, not empty backdrops between famous destinations.
- Label advertisements and sponsored listings. Payment must never buy a false historical claim or hide an unpaid local alternative.
- Collect only the location data needed for the journey feature and explain why it is used.

## 7. Final operational checks — not additional writing

These checks may change visitor cards, but the stop narratives above are frozen:

1. Confirm the current PRASA status of Johannesburg–Cape Town passenger service before every public demo.
2. Capture current Gautrain schedule and fare information on the day of travel or presentation.
3. Verify the Park Station transfer route, assistance points and safe connection time.
4. Confirm current opening hours, fees and accessibility for attractions selected for the live demo.
5. Verify local transfers rather than assuming station walkability.
6. Confirm businesses directly and label them as listings unless a partnership agreement exists.
7. Obtain image licences, photographer credits and consent for oral histories.
