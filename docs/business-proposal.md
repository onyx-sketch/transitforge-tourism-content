# Rails & Roots: tourism and business proposal

**Status: hackathon prototype and proposed business, 23 September 2026.** Rails & Roots has not launched commercially. TransitForge has no confirmed advertising placement, rail-operator partnership, ticketing agreement, paying customers or measured tourism impact.

## The opportunity

South Africans already travel within their own country. Rails & Roots focuses on a narrower question: what if a passenger knew more about the places they passed and could save one for a later visit? The proposed problem is a gap in discovery and understandable journey information. We still need traveller research to measure how many people experience that gap.

The Department of Tourism's Tourism Growth Partnership Plan 2025–2030 identifies domestic destination marketing and the development of routes and experiences as priorities. Rails & Roots is one possible product response, not a programme endorsed by the Department.

## What the traveller gets

Lerato starts at Pretoria Gautrain Station. She chooses the TransitForge journey, follows the operating Gautrain leg to Gautrain Park Station, and learns that Johannesburg Park Station is a separate part of the interchange. She then explores a **simulated** Johannesburg–Cape Town journey based on the historically documented Shosholoza Meyl station sequence. Stories introduce all the named stations; five featured places offer deeper, sourced experiences. Lerato can save places for a future domestic trip.

Rovos Rail and The Blue Train appear as separate operator-listed Pretoria–Cape Town choices. Their published journeys are not the same as the Shosholoza Meyl simulation. Travellers must confirm current itineraries, prices and bookings with each operator.

## How this could support domestic tourism

- Give smaller places visibility as part of a coherent journey, rather than showing only the departure city and destination.
- Help travellers understand the difference between a railway station and a museum, neighbourhood, park or business nearby.
- Let a passenger save an interesting place even when the journey does not allow a visit that day.
- Give local tourism providers a way to be discovered once their details, access and permissions have been verified.

These are proposed mechanisms. The prototype has **not** yet demonstrated more visits, bookings, jobs or spending.

## How people would hear about Rails & Roots

**First: an owned preview.** Publish a mobile-friendly example of Lerato's journey, a short explanation of the Johannesburg interchange, a sample place story and a clear simulation notice. Share the preview through TransitForge's own channels and a QR code.

**Second: community and destination voices.** Invite local museums, guides and tourism organisations to check how their places are represented. Share useful stories with their permission; a mention does not make them a partner.

**Third: proposed station advertising.** Test a paid banner or digital display in the Gautrain station environment. The idea comes from the team's observation of existing platform advertising, including the kind of placement seen at Sandton. JCDecaux lists static and digital advertising opportunities at Gautrain stations. TransitForge would need an actual media quotation, available inventory, creative approval and a budget before placing anything.

**Fourth: proposed website advertising.** Pitch a small, clearly labelled advert linking to the Rails & Roots preview on Gautrain's or PRASA's official website. This requires each organisation's permission, brand and technical review, and an agreement. We have not established that either website offers such an advert slot.

### Proposed advert copy

**Gautrain-area concept**

> Your journey has a story. Discover the places beyond the window with Rails & Roots.
>
> Explore the Pretoria–Cape Town guide.

The landing page must immediately explain that the Gautrain leg is an operating service and the Johannesburg–Cape Town Shosholoza Meyl leg is simulated.

**PRASA-site concept**

> Explore the stories along a historic South African rail journey.
>
> See the Rails & Roots prototype.

This cannot imply that the suspended Johannesburg–Cape Town service is available to book or that PRASA endorses the product.

## How the business could earn money

| Possible revenue | What someone pays for | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Optional premium content | Downloadable audio stories or curated themed collections | Basic route facts and service-status labels stay free |
| Clearly marked promoted listings | Visibility for a verified local tourism business | Paid placement never changes historical facts or becomes an undisclosed recommendation |
| Referral commissions | A qualifying purchase made with a contracted attraction or booking provider | Disclose the arrangement; never imply an unavailable train can be booked |
| Content services | Sourced, maintained route storytelling for an organisation | Requires a signed agreement and clear editorial responsibilities |

None of these streams currently generates revenue. Costs to test include research, writing, translation, audio, image rights, hosting, accessibility, updates, support and advertising. We must compare actual costs and customer interest before claiming the model is profitable.

## What we would test

1. Ask first-time travellers whether the Pretoria–Johannesburg interchange is understandable.
2. Test whether passengers read, listen to or save the place stories.
3. Check whether the five featured stories and supporting profiles represent their communities accurately.
4. Measure preview visits, QR scans, completed route previews, saved places and voluntary feedback.
5. Only after a genuine launch, investigate whether saved places led to real visits. A scan or click is **not** a tourist visit.
