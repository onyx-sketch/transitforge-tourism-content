# Rails & Roots source policy

Every factual claim shown to a traveller must be traceable to a source. Record the source link, what it confirms, and when it was checked.

## Choose evidence for the claim

Use a rail operator's own information for its routes, stations and travel instructions. Use an attraction or institution's own information for its visitor offering. Use historical reports for historical claims. Dated journalism can explain a service change, but keep its publication date visible.

An operator listing a route does not verify a particular departure, fare, platform or stop on a future date.

## Distinguish the places we describe

- **Station:** a documented rail stop.
- **Pass-through place:** a place along a journey without a confirmed passenger stop.
- **Attraction:** a separate place someone could choose to visit.
- **Business:** a provider of food, accommodation or another service.

Do not describe an attraction as walkable from a station without checking the route. Do not imply that a listed business is a TransitForge partner.

## Date changing information

Check service status, schedules, fares, entrances, walking directions, opening hours, prices and accessibility again before using them for real travel advice. If a detail cannot be verified, say so or leave it out.

The 500-metre, six-minute Johannesburg map result measures a route between selected address pins. It is not a verified platform-to-platform transfer time.

## Label the simulation

Lerato's Gautrain leg represents an operating service. Her Johannesburg-to-Cape Town Shosholoza Meyl leg is a simulation based on a historical station sequence. Historical stops do not establish a current timetable or ticket.

## Correcting content

When information changes, update the claim, its source and its checked date together. Preserve older evidence when it is needed to explain the simulated historical route.
