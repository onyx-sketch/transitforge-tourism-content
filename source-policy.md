# Rails & Roots source policy

Rails & Roots helps people understand a journey and discover places in South Africa. Every factual claim shown to a traveller must be traceable to a source. This applies to route information, history, attractions, food, accommodation and practical travel advice.

## Record the source with the claim

Each research record should include:

- A link or clear reference to the source.
- The date the information was checked.
- What the source actually confirms.
- A note when the information is historical, estimated or still unverified.

Prefer the rail operator for its own stations, schedules, fares and service notices; a government body or established institution for its own records; and an attraction or business for its own visitor details. Reporting can provide useful context, but identify it as reporting and record its publication date.

## Keep different kinds of places separate

A **station** is a documented rail stop. A **pass-through place** is somewhere along the journey without a confirmed passenger stop. An **attraction** is a place a traveller might visit separately. A **business** is a place offering a service, such as food or accommodation.

Being near a station does not prove that an attraction is walkable from it. Listing a business does not mean it is a TransitForge partner.

## Treat changing details as dated information

Train status, departure times, fares, entrances, walking directions, opening hours, prices and accessibility can change. Record when each detail was checked and verify it again before presenting it as advice for a real trip. Do not turn one dated journey-planner result into a permanent timetable.

If we cannot verify a detail, leave it out of traveller instructions or label it clearly as unverified.

## Keep Lerato's simulation clear

Lerato's Pretoria-to-Johannesburg Gautrain leg represents an operating service. The Johannesburg-to-Cape Town Shosholoza Meyl leg is a simulation based on a historically documented station sequence. A historical stop is not proof of a current train, timetable, ticket or boarding platform.

The Johannesburg walking estimate in our route record describes a map search between two selected address pins. It does not establish the time needed to change platforms or guarantee a connection.

## Tell tourism stories responsibly

We can feature selected places in depth and give the other stops shorter, sourced context. For each suggested visit, distinguish the story of the place from practical details such as how to get there, entry costs and opening hours. Verify those practical details separately.

Describe domestic tourism as a goal of Rails & Roots. Do not claim that the prototype has already increased visits, bookings or spending unless we collect evidence showing that result.

## Correcting a record

When a source conflicts with an existing record, check its date and what it covers. Update the claim, its source and its checked date together. Keep historical route evidence labelled as historical even when a newer source describes a different service pattern.
